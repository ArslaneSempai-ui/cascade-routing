# Which signals predict an unusable value, without the answer key

Measured 21 August 2026 on rows already recorded. No GPU.

**Denominator, declared once and used identically everywhere below:** every value
a tier produced on the hard corpus, blanks included — **984 values**.

**Target:** anything that is not `clean` — a wrong value and a blank both need
redoing. **594 of 984, 60.4 %.** That is also the precision a randomly firing
signal reaches, which is why the control is not optional.

## The bench is valid

**Oracle signal — the answer key used as a signal: precision 100 %, recall
100 %.** Without it, "no signal separates" and "the harness is broken" would be
the same output, and the first is the likeliest result a measurement like this
produces. It separates perfectly, so the numbers below mean something. This was
missing from the first version of this bench.

## The signals

| signal | fires | precision | recall | false alarms | extra calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ORACLE (positive control) | 594 | 100 % | 100 % | 0 | 0 |
| **blank (baseline)** | 210 | **82.9 %** | 29.3 % | 36 | **0** |
| disagreement | 618 | 82.4 % | **85.7 %** | 109 | **1** |
| shape | 132 | **94.7 %** | 21.0 % | 7 | 0 |
| absent from document | 120 | 92.5 % | 18.7 % | 9 | 0 |
| two of the three | 190 | 93.2 % | 29.8 % | 13 | 1 |
| all three | 32 | **100 %** | 5.4 % | **0** | 1 |

All beat a random signal firing at the same rate.

## Two things the first version of this bench got wrong

**The blank baseline is itself a strong signal, and it is free.** A blank output
is unusable 82.9 % of the time — because on this corpus a blank is sometimes the
correct answer. Every signal must beat *this*, not merely beat chance.

**Disagreement does not beat it, and costs a call.** 82.4 % precision against the
baseline's 82.9 %, for one extra tier invocation per value. Knowing that `gen-4b`
and `gen-8b` diverge means running both — paying for the expensive tier to
discover you needed the expensive tier. Its recall is much better, 85.7 % against
29.3 %, so it is not useless; but as a precision signal it buys nothing it did
not already have for free.

**Shape and absence cost nothing and beat the baseline on precision** — 94.7 %
and 92.5 % against 82.9 %, both computed from the document and the returned
string alone. These are the two that transpose without a second tier.

## What escalation would actually return

"The cheap one is wrong" is not the number. If the expensive one is also wrong,
escalating costs and returns nothing. Only the crossing counts.

| cheap → expensive | cheap fails | expensive saves | wasted escalations |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gen-0.6b` → `gen-4b` | 81 | 17 (21 %) | 64 |
| `gen-4b` → `gen-8b` | 69 | 17 (25 %) | 52 |
| `large` → `gen-4b` | 118 | **51 (43 %)** | 67 |

Escalating on every failure of `gen-4b` would invoke `gen-8b` sixty-nine times to
rescue seventeen values. Three escalations in four buy nothing.

## Signal-guided escalation against a random router at equal spend

The control that matters: escalate the *same number* of cases, chosen at random.
If the signal does not beat that, the gain was bought by spending more, not by
deciding better.

| pair | guided | gains | random at equal spend | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gen-0.6b` → `gen-4b` | 72 | 16 | 7.2 | beats |
| `gen-4b` → `gen-8b` | 39 | 12 | 4.0 | beats |
| `large` → `gen-4b` | **29** | **16** | 8.6 | beats |

All three beat the matched random router, by a factor of two to three. The best
is `large → gen-4b`: twenty-nine guided escalations recover sixteen values, where
spending the same at random recovers under nine.

So the signal does decide better, not merely spend more. That is the finding.

## Model-emitted confidence: 0 of 3 tiers expose one

Zero found is not zero looked for. The generation call neither requests nor keeps
token log-probabilities, so no tier exposes a confidence today. Obtaining it means
re-measuring the generative tiers while storing them — about five minutes for
`gen-4b` alone, forty for all three. It is the only one of the four signal
families that cannot be answered from stored rows.

## Reservations, beside the numbers rather than under them

**Thirty to forty-four hard documents.** Enough to rule out a signal that does
not beat chance; not enough to estimate a precision. Per-field intervals on the
best signal are wider than the gaps between signals, so read this as "shape and
absence work and cost nothing", not as an ordering between them.

**The base rate here is 60.4 %, on deliberately broken documents.** On clean
traffic it is far lower, and every precision falls with it. What transfers is
that these signals separate at all, not the numbers.

**A threshold tuned on this corpus would use the very data that bounds its
interval.** On these counts, per-document routing may be unvalidatable on the
available data even if it works. That is not a reason to call it good, nor bad.

**The signals never need the key. Only their validation does.** All are computed
from what a client already has. The key enters only here, to find out whether
they are worth anything.
