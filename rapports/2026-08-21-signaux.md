# Which signals predict a wrong value, without the answer key

Measured 21 August 2026 on rows already recorded. No GPU.

**810 scored values** — the six tiers over the hard corpus, blanks excluded. A
blank is already visible and needs no signal; including them would inflate every
score with the easy case.

**Base error rate 51.9 %.** That is also the precision a randomly firing signal
reaches, which is why the control below matters.

## The three signals, and what each costs

| signal | fires | precision | recall | false alarms | random control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| disagreement | 444/810 | 75.4 % | **79.8 %** | 109 | 51.8 % |
| shape | 155/810 | 80.7 % | 29.8 % | 30 | 52.0 % |
| **absent from the document** | 120/810 | **92.5 %** | 26.4 % | **9** | 51.9 % |
| any of the three | 474/810 | 76.4 % | 86.2 % | 112 | 51.9 % |
| two of the three | 213/810 | 83.1 % | 42.1 % | 36 | 51.7 % |
| all three | 32/810 | **100 %** | 7.6 % | **0** | 51.3 % |

All six beat a random signal firing at the same rate. None is noise.

**Precision** is the share of firings that really are wrong. **Recall** is the
share of all wrong values caught. **False alarms** are correct values sent for
review for nothing, out of 390 correct ones — that is what the client pays.

## What each one is

**Disagreement** — the value differs from what the plurality of the other tiers
returns for the same field. Best recall by a distance: it catches four wrong
values in five. It also fires on more than half of everything and sends 109
correct values to review, so it is a wide net, not a verdict.

**Shape** — the value fails a per-field rule declared in the code: a birth date
carries a four-digit year, a country has no digits and at most four words, an
address carries a number or a comma. Declared, not learned from the key.

**Absent from the document** — the returned string does not occur in the source
text. The best of the three by a distance on what it costs: 92.5 % precision for
nine false alarms in 390. On cut documents the models invent, and an invention
usually does not appear in the text it was invented from.

**All three together fire on 32 values and every one of them is wrong**, with no
false alarm at all. That is a small, exact trigger — an escalation rule that
never wastes a review, and catches one wrong value in thirteen.

## Why this transposes to a client

**The signal never needs the key. Only its validation does.** All three are
computed from what a client already has: the document, the returned value, and
for the first a second opinion. The key enters only here, to find out whether
they are worth anything.

That is the whole reason this is worth measuring rather than assuming.

## Two reservations, which belong beside the numbers

**Thirty to forty-four hard documents.** Enough to rule out a signal that does
not beat chance — all six clear it by twenty-five points or more — and not enough
to estimate any of their precisions. A confidence interval on these counts would
be wider than the gaps between the signals, so the ordering above should be read
as "all three work", not as "absence beats shape".

**Measured on broken documents only.** The base error rate here is 51.9 %; on the
clean corpus it is around 8 %. A signal's precision falls with the base rate, so
none of these numbers transfers to clean traffic unchanged. What transfers is
that they separate at all.

## What it unblocks, and what it does not

Escalation, abstention and per-document routing all needed exactly one thing: a
usable "not sure here" without a key. There is one, and the strictest version
costs nothing in wasted reviews.

What it does not settle is whether escalation pays. That depends on the price of
a review against the price of a second tier, and both are the client's numbers,
not ours. The measurement gives the rates; the trade is theirs.

## The fourth signal, not measured

Model-emitted confidence would need a re-run: the generation call currently
returns text only, so token log-probabilities are not stored. Adding them means
re-measuring every generative tier — about forty minutes for the three, or five
for `gen-4b` alone. It is the only one of the four that cannot be answered from
stored rows.
