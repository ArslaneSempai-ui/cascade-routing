# The escalation curve

**It never leaves zero.** At every budget, on both ladders, guided escalation
completes no more whole documents than the fixed routing it started from — and a
better fixed routing completes two, at half the price, at every point on the
curve.

Measured 21 August 2026 from stored rows. No GPU.

## The ranking, which is free and measured monotone

The number of agreeing signals is an ordering, so escalation candidates can be
sorted from most certain to least and stopped at a budget. Measured on the free
signals only:

| agreeing signals | cases | unusable |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 32 | **100 %** |
| 1 | 398 | 86.9 % |
| 0 | 554 | 39.0 % |

**Disagreement between tiers is excluded from the ranking**, though it appears in
the signal bench. Computing it means running a second tier, so it cannot decide
whether to run a second tier. The three that remain — blank, implausible shape,
value absent from the document — cost nothing.

**The 100 % is thirty-two draws, not a guarantee.** It is written here because a
rate over thirty-two cases carries an interval wide enough to matter and this
whole table rests on it.

## The curve

30 documents, 150 fields. Budget is a share of fields, set from outside.

**Escalate one step**

| budget | escalations | whole docs | random | oracle | €/1000 | ms/doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 % | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.84 | 968 |
| 3 % | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.89 | 1,149 |
| 10 % | 14 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.91 | 1,224 |
| 20 % | 28 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.94 | 1,332 |
| all | 90 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2.12 | 1,889 |

**Escalate to `gen-8b`**

| budget | escalations | whole docs | random | oracle | €/1000 | ms/doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 % | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.84 | 968 |
| 3 % | 4 | 0 | 0.03 | 0 | 1.89 | 1,149 |
| 10 % | 15 | 0 | 0.12 | 0 | 2.02 | 1,563 |
| 20 % | 30 | 0 | 0.20 | **1** | 2.20 | 2,140 |
| all | 103 | **1** | 0.69 | 1 | 3.10 | 4,960 |

**Best fixed routing at the same cost, at every one of these points: 2 whole
documents for 0.99 €/1000.**

## Where it flattens is nowhere, because it never rises

The question was where the curve flattens. It does not flatten — it never leaves
the floor. Spending the entire escalation budget, on the better of the two
ladders, buys one whole document for 68 % more money and five times the latency.

**And the oracle caps at one.** Perfect foresight about which fields to escalate,
at any budget, on either ladder. So this is not a failure of the signal, of the
ordering, or of the budget: escalating to a better tier simply does not complete
documents on this corpus.

## What does work is a better first call

The recommended routing sends three of five fields to `rules`:

    name→large  birth→rules  document→rules  country→rules  address→gen-4b

On broken documents `rules` returns a blank 133 times in 164, so nothing comes out
whole. The best fixed routing inside the same budget is entirely generative:

    name→gen-0.6b  birth→gen-8b  document→gen-4b  country→gen-4b  address→gen-0.6b

Two whole documents, 0.99 € per thousand — **half the price of the recommended
routing, and better**. It also beats `gen-8b` used alone, which completes one.
Mixing tiers per field is what pays here. Doing it per document is what does not.

## The reservation that governs the whole table

**Counts of zero, one and two out of thirty.** This repository does not publish a
rate below twenty successes. Nothing in these tables is distinguishable from
anything else in them.

So this is not "escalation loses by one document". It is: **at these counts no
version of escalation shows a gain, on either ladder, at any budget, even with
perfect foresight** — and a larger hard corpus could overturn every line. What
does not depend on the counts is the shape of the failure: the dominant error on
this corpus is the blank, escalation cannot repair a routing whose first call is
wrong, and the money spent on second calls buys more when spent on first ones.

## Two sources, kept apart

Correctness comes from the hard-corpus rows. Price and latency come from the
published relevé's latencies, taken on an idle machine.
