# The escalation curve, under two budgets

**Escalating one field to `gen-8b` breaks the declared latency ceiling.** Not
"costs too much" — breaks it. And every cheaper escalation target that fits under
the ceiling completes zero whole documents, including with perfect foresight.

Measured 21 August 2026 from stored rows. No GPU.

## The budget, declared before the measurement

    corpus budget      10 % of fields
    per-document cap    2 escalated fields
    latency ceiling  2,000 ms per document

Written before the results, because a curve with several points offers several
chances to clear a bar where a single recommendation offered one. The condition
is read at the declared budget, never at the most flattering point.

## Why one budget was not enough

A corpus budget bounds cost. It does not bound latency, because **escalations
arrive in clusters**: a hard document is hard on several fields at once, and
ranking by signal count makes the clustering worse rather than diluting it.

At the "at least one signal" step, **all thirty documents escalate at least two
fields and fifteen escalate four or five.** A 68 % corpus budget is not 68 %
spread thinly; it is nearly every field of every document.

That is why the previous version of this table was misleading. It reported mean
latency per document — 1,149 ms against a 2,000 ms ceiling, comfortable — and the
mean hid that every escalated document was over.

## The curve has two steps, not a continuum

| step | fields | share of corpus |
|---|---|---|
| score ≥ 2 | 4 | 2.7 % |
| score ≥ 1 | 103 | 68.7 % |

There is nothing between. A client asking for 5 % gets 2.7 %; a client asking for
20 % gets 2.7 % as well. Effective rates are reported beside requested ones.

## The arithmetic that settles it

Base routing: 968 ms per document. Ceiling: 2,000 ms. One escalated field costs:

| target | document total | |
|---|---|---|
| `small` | 986 ms | fits |
| `gen-0.6b` | 1,190 ms | fits |
| `gen-4b` | 1,677 ms | fits |
| **`gen-8b`** | **2,069 ms** | **over** |

So escalation to the only tier accurate enough to matter is **inadmissible under
the declared latency ceiling**, for a single field, on any document. And
escalation to the tiers that do fit returns zero whole documents at every budget,
with the oracle, on the previous measurement.

That is a cleaner result than "escalation does not pay". The escalation that fits
the budget returns nothing; the escalation that could return something is not
allowed to run.

## What still works, and it is not a cascade

The best fixed routing inside the same cost — `name→gen-0.6b`, `birth→gen-8b`,
`document→gen-4b`, `country→gen-4b`, `address→gen-0.6b` — completes two documents
at 0.99 € per thousand, half the recommended routing's price. It spends the money
on the first call instead of the second.

## The reservation that governs the counts

**Zero, one and two whole documents out of thirty**, against a floor of twenty
below which this repository publishes no rate. Nothing in the whole-document
column is distinguishable from anything else in it.

The latency finding does not depend on those counts. It is arithmetic over
measured per-field latencies, and it holds whatever the accuracy turns out to be.
